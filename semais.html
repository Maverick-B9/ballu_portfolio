SEMAIS

A self-evolving simulation engine where a population of agents learns to coordinate under constraints, changing environments and adversarial pressure.

01THE QUESTION

How can multiple agents coordinate under constraints, changing environments and adversarial pressure?

Hand-written rules for each agent do not scale to that question. Behaviour that works in one environment fails when the environment shifts or an opponent adapts. SEMAIS asks a different question: instead of designing the behaviour, can a population discover it?

02THE APPROACH

I built a self-evolving multi-agent simulation engine in Python. Each agent carries a 7-parameter behavioural genome. A genetic algorithm evolves that genome over 50+ generations across 100+ agents.

Agents navigate with A* pathfinding and talk to each other over fault-tolerant communication, so one failed link does not stall the group. Afterwards, unsupervised clustering groups the evolved genomes into 4–5 distinct strategies.

03HOW IT
WORKS

A simplified view of the flow, step by step.

  1. Spawn

    A population of 100+ agents, each with its own 7-parameter genome.

  2. Act

    Agents path-find with A* and exchange messages over fault-tolerant links.

  3. Select

    The genetic algorithm keeps the genomes that performed best.

  4. Evolve

    Selected genomes are recombined and mutated into the next generation.

LOOPRepeat for 50+ generations.
THENCluster the final genomes into 4–5 emergent strategies.
